BasicAI — turn sequence
=========================

This describes what BasicAI.run_turn() actually does, in order, as a
reference for discussing/tuning its behavior. Each step is labeled to make
it easy to refer to ("step 2", "the safe-kill check", etc.) when requesting
changes.

Step 0 — Game-over guard
-------------------------
Before and after every step below, the AI checks sandbox._game_over and
bails out immediately if the game has ended (e.g. a hero died mid-turn).
This isn't really a "behavior" step, just a safety check so a stray AI
coroutine can't keep acting after the match is over.

Step 1 — Place a resource
---------------------------
If the AI has MORE THAN ONE card in hand, it picks a random card from
among its hand EXCLUDING any Protector or Pet (subtype contains "Pet") to
place face-down as a resource. (If it has only one card left, it skips
this — otherwise it would strand itself with no card in hand for the rest
of the turn.) Protectors and Pets are excluded because they're too
valuable to bury face-down — Protectors are the deck's defensive core (see
DeckManager_behavior.txt), and Pets aren't always Protectors but are
usually otherwise valuable allies. If every remaining card in hand is a
Protector or a Pet, the AI skips placing a resource entirely that turn
rather than burning one of them.

Step 2 — Summon allies
------------------------
Repeatedly: look at all Ally cards in hand, sort by cost (most expensive
first), and play the most expensive one the AI can currently afford. Repeat
until no affordable ally is left in hand. (Instant Ally cards — see "Known
gaps" below — are played the same as any other ally, on the AI's own turn
only; the AI has no concept of flashing one in on the opponent's turn.)

Step 2.5 — Activate powers
-----------------------------
Repeatedly, until no usable power remains: look at every card the AI
controls (heroes included) with an "activate" power that's currently legal
(can_use_power_reason == "") AND has estimated value > 0 according to
AIAdvisor.estimate_power_value (see logic/AIAdvisor.gd). Powers with
value 0 are skipped entirely — e.g. a heal power when nobody is damaged,
or a discard power when the opponent's hand is empty.

Among the remaining candidates, pick the one with the highest estimated
cost (AIAdvisor.estimate_power_cost — sums resource/flip cost tokens,
using a power's minimum for an "X" spec; self_damage doesn't count),
random tie-break. Activate it (this may itself prompt for a target — see
"Targeting heuristics" below — resolved instantly with no UI).

If a power resolves but finds no legal target/sacrifice (committed ==
false, cost refunded), that card is not retried again this turn — the
fizzle guard prevents an infinite loop for edge cases the advisor missed.

Step 3 — Attack
-----------------
Ready attackers with 0 ATK are excluded entirely — they can never deal
lethal or even meaningful damage, so they're skipped rather than thrown
away on a pointless attack.

Repeatedly, until no ready (ATK > 0) attacker has any legal target left:

  Step 3a — Lethal check (hero face)
  Before any other targeting logic, sum the ATK (including combat bonuses)
  of every ready attacker that can legally target the enemy hero. If that
  total >= the hero's current HP, commit ALL of them to the hero's face
  immediately — sorted highest ATK first so lethal is secured as early as
  possible in the sequence. The game-over guard after each combat handles
  the hero dying mid-sequence cleanly. If this fires, the loop restarts
  from step 3a with the updated board state (which will exit on game-over
  or proceed to 3b/3c if the hero somehow survived via protection).

  Step 3b — Safe-kill check
  Look at every (ready attacker, legal target) pair — legality is checked
  per-attacker, not precomputed once for the whole turn, since some
  restrictions (e.g. Sarmoth's "can attack only this if able") can make a
  target legal for one attacker but not another. A pair is a "safe kill" if:
    - attacker.atk >= target.current_health   (the attack is lethal), AND
    - attacker.current_health > target.atk    (the attacker survives the
                                                 retaliation)
  If one or more safe kills exist, pick the attacker with the highest
  current HP among them. Attack with that attacker against its safe-kill
  target.

  Step 3c — Regular attack (fallback)
  If no safe kill exists, pick a random ready attacker that has at least
  one legal target, then attack a random legal target for that specific
  attacker (no further strategy).

  Whichever sub-step fired, the attack resolves (protect point, then
  wiggle + damage — see "Targeting heuristics" below for how a defending
  AI handles a protect-point prompt), then the loop re-evaluates from 3a
  again with the updated board state.

Step 4 — End turn
-------------------
Once no ready attacker / legal target combination remains, end the turn
(same call a human's "End Turn" button makes).


Notes for future tuning
-------------------------
- "Legal attacker" / "legal target" already route through DuelTable's rule
  hooks (can_propose_attacker / _is_valid_target -> can_propose_defender)
  when running on Duel Table, so the AI automatically respects turn
  ownership, summoning sickness (and Ferocity), Elusive, and attack-locking
  effects like Sarmoth's without any AI-specific code for those rules. On
  Sandbox, those hooks are permissive, so the AI behaves the same as a
  human would in Sandbox (no restrictions beyond "not currently exhausted").
- Step 2 has no analogous "best play" logic yet — it's purely
  most-expensive-affordable-first. Worth revisiting once cost efficiency /
  board state evaluation becomes a goal.
- Pet/uniqueness violations triggered by the AI's own plays are resolved
  automatically via BasicAI.choose_discard() (lowest current HP, then
  cheapest cost, then random) — this happens inside
  SandboxTable._check_violations_for(), not inside run_turn() itself, but
  it's part of the AI's overall decision-making and worth knowing about
  when discussing AI behavior as a whole.

Known gaps
------------
- Instant Ally / instant abilities (subtype contains "Instant") are not
  played at instant speed by the AI — it only plays cards during its own
  turn, in step 2, like any other card. It's capable of playing such a
  card, just not of using its instant-speed timing window.


Targeting heuristics
=======================
Whenever a power or combat effect needs to choose a target/card and the
deciding player is AI, SandboxTable._prompt_target() resolves it instantly
via _ai_pick_target() (or, for lethal-aware damage, the more specific
_ai_pick_damage_target()) instead of showing UI. None of these are meant to
be "smart" — only good enough that the AI can use any fully-implemented
card; tuning them further isn't a current priority. Picked by cursor_kind:

  "damage" (_ai_pick_damage_target)
    1. Prefer the opposing side; fall back to all candidates if the
       opposing side has none (e.g. an unusual target_scope).
    2. Lethal enemy HERO takes priority over everything else — if any
       opposing hero is in lethal range, kill it.
    3. Otherwise, among all candidates this hit would kill, prefer the
       most expensive one (a kill is a kill, but denying a bigger
       investment is better); random tie-break.
    4. Otherwise (nothing lethal), pick randomly among the opposing side.
    Note: this heuristic needs the damage amount before it can tell what's
    "lethal," so it can't be used for effects where the amount is only
    determined AFTER the target is chosen (e.g. Dizdemona's/Radak's
    self-cost/sacrifice-funded X) — those fall back to a simpler heuristic
    instead (effectively cursor_kind "opponent" / random).

  "heal"
    Prefer the AI's own side. Sort by biggest missing HP (max_health -
    current_health) descending — a tankier character that's taken more
    total damage is a better target than a naturally-low-HP one that's
    only down by a little. Heroes are included automatically whenever
    they're a legal candidate for that particular power.

  "sacrifice" / "discard"
    De-prioritize Protectors first — losing one weakens the defensive
    core the deck is built around. Pets are NOT de-prioritized here
    (unlike resource placement): a cheap Pet with no special effect
    isn't inherently more valuable than a cheap generic ally, so it
    just falls into the normal cheapest-cost ordering.
    Among non-Protector candidates, prefer cheapest cost to minimize
    the loss. If the entire hand is Protectors, falls back to cheapest
    among those — discard is mandatory.

  "protect" (protect-point prompt, see Protector below)
    Only ever called with a non-empty candidate list (the AI skips the
    prompt entirely if it has no ready Protector to offer — see below), so
    this heuristic always protects when reached. Picks the candidate with
    the highest current HP (more likely to survive this combat and be
    worth healing later).

  "opponent" (choosing which PLAYER something happens to, not which card —
  e.g. Mias the Putrid's "target player discards")
    Prefer the opponent, never self.

  (anything else)
    Random choice among all candidates.

Other AI decision points (not routed through _ai_pick_target)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  X value (BasicAI.choose_x_value)
    Always spends the maximum legal X — more value (bigger heal/damage/
    etc.) is strictly better with no other tradeoff currently modeled.

  Uniqueness-violation discard (BasicAI.choose_discard)
    Lowest current HP first (cull the weakened one), then cheapest cost
    (keep the more powerful one), then random.

  Power activation priority (BasicAI.estimate_power_cost — see step 2.5)
    Highest estimated resource cost first, random tie-break.

  Protect point (see Protector, 602.2)
    A defending AI ALWAYS protects if it has at least one ready ally with
    Protector (other than the proposed defender itself) — it never
    declines on purpose, since right-click-to-decline is a human-only
    interaction. If it has no ready Protector available (none in play, or
    all currently exhausted), it has nothing to decide and skips straight
    to the original defender, same as if no Protector existed. Which
    protector it picks, when it has a choice, follows the "protect"
    heuristic above.
